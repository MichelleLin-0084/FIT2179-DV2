Australia is one of the most disaster-prone nations on Earth. From the flooding plains of Queensland to the wildfire corridors of Victoria, natural hazards reshape communities every year. This visualisation maps where disasters strike, what they cost, and who bears the burden — told through data spanning more than two decades.

01 — Overview

The scale of the problem

Since 2000, Australia has recorded over 130 natural disaster events significant enough to appear in the global EM-DAT database. Floods and storms dominate in frequency, but wildfires have caused some of the most devastating single events.

02 — Geography

Where disasters strike

No part of Australia is immune. The north is battered by cyclones and floods, the south-east by heat and fire, and the interior by drought. The maps below reveal the spatial fingerprint of each disaster type across the continent.

03 — Patterns

Seasonal rhythms & long-term trends

Natural disasters are not random — they follow Australia's seasons. Cyclones track the wet season in the north, while bushfires peak in the southern summer. Understanding these patterns is critical for preparedness.
